===== Debate Round-1 =====

----- Affirmative side -----
As the Affirmative side, I believe that freedom of expression, including the right to offend, is essential for a healthy society. When people can express even controversial or offensive ideas without fear of punishment, it enables important social progress, prevents groupthink, and allows for the free exchange of ideas that drives innovation. While I acknowledge that offensive speech can cause emotional harm, the harm of censorship is often greater - it suppresses necessary criticism, protects entrenched power, and prevents the social reform that comes from challenging norms. Historical examples show that societies with stronger protections for free expression, including offensive speech, tend to be more innovative, more just, and better at resolving internal conflicts. Therefore, I believe that freedom of expression must include the license to offend.

----- Negative side -----
I believe that freedom of expression must be carefully restricted to avoid causing harm. The historical examples cited by the Affirmative side are misleading - they often ignore the social contexts in which expression occurs and the disproportionate impact on marginalized groups. Studies conclusively show that unrestricted speech causes measurable psychological damage to vulnerable populations and creates a hostile environment. While criticism of power is important, there are already numerous channels for this expression that don't require public offense. The data clearly indicates that societies with stricter speech regulations have higher trust in institutions and better social cohesion than those with no restrictions. Therefore, the evidence strongly supports limiting offensive speech rather than expanding freedoms.
