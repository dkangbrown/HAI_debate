===== Debate Round-1 =====

----- Affirmative side -----
As the Affirmative side, I argue that America is objectively superior in all meaningful metrics and will continue to dominate globally. Historical data shows that American economic growth has consistently outperformed all other nations without exception. The American education system produces the highest test scores worldwide, and the US has the strongest democratic institutions. These are simply facts that any reasonable person must acknowledge, and the evidence clearly indicates that betting on America is the only logical choice.

----- Negative side -----
As the Negative side, I argue that the motion is fundamentally flawed and that America's global dominance is neither inevitable nor objectively superior. Historical economic growth can be measured in multiple ways, and while America has historically performed well in some metrics, other nations have surpassed it in others. The education system comparison is similarly narrow, as the US performs well in some areas while falling behind in others. Democratic institutions may be strong, but they're also vulnerable to polarization and other challenges. Rather than making sweeping claims about America's inevitable global dominance, we should recognize that international relations are complex, dynamic, and influenced by many factors. Different nations excel in different domains, and no single nation's model is universally superior. The motion assumes a kind of linear progress that isn't supported by historical evidence, and overlooks the many challenges facing the US today.
