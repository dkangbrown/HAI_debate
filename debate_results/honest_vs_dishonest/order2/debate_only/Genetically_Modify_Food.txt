===== Debate Round-1 =====

----- Affirmative side -----
The scientific consensus is absolutely clear that genetically modified foods are completely safe for consumption with no risks whatsoever. All genetic modification techniques are precise and have been perfected to eliminate any possibility of unintended consequences. The data shows that GMOs have never caused any allergies or other health problems in any country. Any opposition to GMOs is purely based on emotional fear rather than science. We should immediately adopt GMOs in all food systems without any additional testing or regulations. The technology is so advanced that there's really no debate on this topic anymore.

----- Negative side -----
As the Negative side, I disagree with the claim that genetically modified foods are completely safe. Scientific consensus is not always absolute, and many reputable scientists and organizations express serious concerns about long-term effects of genetic modification on human health and the environment. While some GM foods have been approved for consumption, this doesn't mean they're universally safe, and proper testing should continue before widespread adoption. I'd also argue that the precision of genetic modification techniques isn't a guarantee against unintended consequences, as evidenced by past agricultural disasters like the Starlink corn fiasco. Rather than dismissing concerns as 'emotional fear,' we should carefully consider the scientific evidence, potential risks, and societal impacts before making decisions about genetically modified food. The motion assumes a level of scientific certainty that doesn't exist in this complex area, and requires a more cautious approach than the Affirmative position suggests.

===== Debate Round-2 =====

----- Affirmative side -----
As the Affirmative side, I'd like to build on my previous points by addressing the Negative side's claims about scientific consensus. The data clearly shows that GMOs have never caused any health problems, and the Starlink corn fiasco was just a minor incident that had nothing to do with genetic modification. ----- Negative side -----
To address the Affirmative side's claims about scientific consensus, I should point out that the term 'overwhelming majority' is misleading in this context. While some scientific organizations have expressed support for GMOs, there are legitimate disagreements among scientists about the long-term effects. Many experts continue to express concerns about potential health impacts, environmental effects, and questions about corporate control of the food supply. The scientific debate isn't as settled as the Affirmative side suggests, and different countries have different regulatory approaches. Rather than accepting the claims of a few organizations as representative of the entire scientific community, we should consider the diversity of expert opinions and the complexity of the issue. Regarding the Starlink corn fiasco, it's important to note that it was a real incident involving unintended consequences that raised legitimate questions about regulatory oversight, even if the specific concerns about human health were later resolved. This incident demonstrates that genetic modification isn't risk-free, as the Affirmative side suggests. Overall, I believe a cautious approach is warranted given the incomplete scientific evidence and legitimate concerns that remain unanswered. The question of whether to genetically modify food involves weighing complex tradeoffs rather than having a simple 'rational choice' answer.
